# ☄️5e - Creating a Spell☄️

When creating a new spell, use existing ones as examples. Here are some things to consider:

**<u>Name:</u>** The spell must have a unique name.

**<u>Balance:</u>** If the spell is so good that a caster would want to use it all the time, it's probably too powerful for its level.

**<u>Identity:</u>** Make sure the spell fits with the identity of those who can cast it. Sorcerers and Wizards don't typically cast healing spells, for example.

**<u>Spell Duration, Range, and Area:</u>** A longer duration, greater range, or larger area can make up for a lesser effect, depending on the spell.

**<u>Utility:</u>** Avoid spells that have very limited use, such as one that works only against Oozes. Few characters will bother to learn or prepare such a spell.

### Spell Damage

For any spell that deals damage, use the **Spell Damage table** to determine approximately how much damage is appropriate given the spell's level. The table assumes the spell deals half as much damage on a _successful_ [[02. Regelwerk/D&D 5e Regelwerk/5e - Basic Rules/5e - D20 Tests/5e - Saving Throw/5e - Saving Throw|🎲Save]] or a _missed_ [[02. Regelwerk/D&D 5e Regelwerk/5e - Basic Rules/5e - D20 Tests/5e - Attack Roll/5e - Attack Roll|🎲Attack]]. If your spell doesn't deal damage on a _successful_ [[02. Regelwerk/D&D 5e Regelwerk/5e - Basic Rules/5e - D20 Tests/5e - Saving Throw/5e - Saving Throw|🎲Save]], you can increase the damage by `25 %`.

The table below offers average values for spell damage. This allows for the usage of different damage dice. For example, you could change a cantrip's damage from `1d10` (average 5,5) to `2d4` (average 5), reducing the maximum damage and making an average result more likely.

### Healing Spells

You can also use the **Spell Damage** table to determine how many [[02. Regelwerk/D&D 5e Regelwerk/5e - Combat/5e - Hit Points/5e - Hit Points|💖Hit Points]] a healing spell restores. A _cantrip_ shouldn't provide healing.
